## Thesis

The speed of light is the same for all observers; the measurements of time and distance must change accordingly.

## The main ideas

- The Michelson-Morley experiment shows that the speed of light is independent of the motion of the source or the observer.
- Time and distance are relative; simultaneity is not absolute.
- The Lorentz transformation relates the coordinates of two observers in relative motion.
- Mass and energy are equivalent; E = mc².

## Special relativity

Feynman presents the postulates and derives the consequences. The mechanism is that the invariance of the speed of light requires the mixing of space and time coordinates. The implication is that the classical notions of absolute time and absolute length are approximations that hold only at low speeds.
