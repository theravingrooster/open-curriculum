Mission

To make the best insights from the best books free and accessible to everyone.

I did everything I was supposed to do. I went to a competitive high school, worked for the grades, and got into Stanford. For three years I kept doing the same thing: the right classes, the right assignments, the right exams.

Then I realized I wasn't learning. I had become good at school: studying what would be tested, writing what would be graded, and moving on when the quarter ended. Very little of it changed how I thought or what I could do.

So I took my education into my own hands. I started choosing my own books, on how to think, how markets work, how power works, the body, and how to live, and following each one to the next. Nobody assigned them and nobody graded them, and they taught me more than anything I had studied for a grade.

They opened my eyes to what real knowledge can do. Nearly every idea worth learning was first written down by the person who worked it out, and almost all of it is available to anyone, for almost nothing. A degree certifies time spent in a place. It doesn't certify understanding, and understanding was there the whole time.

The obstacle is time. A serious reading list takes years, and most books spend three hundred pages on thirty pages of ideas. The Open Curriculum is the library I wish I had started with. Each guide states the book's argument, the ideas that change decisions, the evidence behind them, and where they fail. Nothing stays unless it carries an idea, so you can start where the best thinkers left off and go further.

The list is short and ordered. Books are chosen for how much they change the way you think, not for how popular they are.

Free. No account, no ads, no certificate. The knowledge is the credential.
