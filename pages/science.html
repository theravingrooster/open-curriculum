The Feynman Lectures on Physics

The full text of all three volumes is free to read online. Each chapter has its own guide page that states the central claim and links to the official lecture.

Richard Feynman taught Caltech’s introductory physics course from 1961 to 1963. Robert B. Leighton and Matthew Sands edited the recorded lectures into three volumes. The New Millennium Edition, corrected and typeset for the web, is available at no charge from The Feynman Lectures on Physics website, run by Caltech with the editors Michael A. Gottlieb and Rudolf Pfeiffer. The site also holds the original lecture recordings, photographs of the blackboards, Feynman’s handwritten notes, and a problem-solving supplement.

The lectures are free to read in a browser. The posting does not transfer any right to download or redistribute the text. The Open Curriculum chapter guides distill the central claim of each lecture; the links send you to the source.

The three volumes

  • Volume I — Mainly Mechanics, Radiation, and Heat (52 chapters).
  • Volume II — Mainly Electromagnetism and Matter (42 chapters).
  • Volume III — Quantum Mechanics (21 chapters).

Supporting materials on the same site

  • Original lecture recordings — the unedited tapes of the 1961–63 course, with links that open the matching chapter.
  • Feynman’s Tips on Physics — four review lectures and selected exercises that fill the gap between ideas and problem-solving.
  • Exercises — problems drawn from the course and later collections, with solutions where available.
  • Original course handouts — the mimeographed notes, quizzes, and lab materials Feynman’s students actually received.

The lectures are demanding. Feynman himself judged that he had not done well by most of his students. They work best for readers who already know some calculus and who treat them as a way of seeing rather than as a course in solving problems. The free official text, the recordings, and the Tips on Physics together form a complete free physics curriculum.
