Method

Read faster without pretending you absorbed it.

A practice note. Speed is a tool. It is not the education.

This library is long on purpose. If you read every page at a spoken pace, you will stall. You do not need a course. You need a pacer, fewer regressions, and honest reps.

The basic science

Most adults read around 200–250 words per minute because they do three expensive things at once:

  • Subvocalization. You hear the words in your head. That caps speed near talking speed.
  • Regression. The eyes jump backward to reread a line that was already fine. Habit, not necessity.
  • Tiny fixations. You land on almost every word instead of taking in a chunk.

The eyes can take in more than one word per stop. Comprehension does not require saying each word internally. What it does require is attention. If you “speed read” while tired and skip the argument, you only collected pages.

Jim Kwik’s method, stripped of the brand

Kwik’s useful core is old visual-pacing work, not magic:

  1. Use a finger, pen, or cursor as a pacer. Move it slightly faster than comfort. The eyes follow motion better than they invent a path.
  2. Widen the take. Look at groups of words, not each syllable. Peripheral vision is already doing this; you have been ignoring it.
  3. Cut the inner narrator down, not out. You can understand “interest rates rose” without pronouncing every phoneme.
  4. Do not reread on instinct. Finish the paragraph. If you actually lost the claim, go back once, on purpose.
  5. Train with a timer. Speed that you cannot repeat tomorrow is a stunt.

That is the whole method. Everything else is motivation wrapped around those five moves.

A free tool: Spreeder

Spreeder is a free RSVP trainer — rapid serial visual presentation. It flashes words or short chunks at a set words-per-minute so you cannot regress and cannot linger. Use it as a gym, not as the way you read Thucydides.

  • Paste a public-domain page or your own notes. Do not paste a book you do not have the rights to reproduce.
  • Start near your current speed. Nudge up 20–30 wpm when comprehension still holds.
  • Then go back to paper or a normal screen with a finger pacer. The tool teaches the tempo. The book still has to be read as a book.
What worked here: the pacer plus Spreeder drills, done often enough to be boring. It took practice. Comprehension dipped at first, then returned. Sustained speed settled above 300 words per minute on ordinary nonfiction — not on poetry, not on a proof, not on a first pass through a hostile argument. Dense books still get a slow pass. The point of speed is to earn more slow passes on the books that deserve them.

What not to do

  • Do not “photograph” pages. That is a sales pitch.
  • Do not race Marcus Aurelius or a balance sheet. Character and accounts are not content mills.
  • Do not skip the one-pager in How to read. Fast input with no output is tourism.

A simple week

  1. Ten minutes of Spreeder on easy prose.
  2. One chapter of the current track book with a finger under the line.
  3. Five sentences in your own words: claim, mechanism, what would change if it were true.

Do that for a month before you decide whether you “can” or “cannot” read faster. The limit is usually patience, not eyesight.
