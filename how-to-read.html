Method

Do not collect books. Extract models.

A short operating manual for this library.

If the bottleneck is pace, not will: see How to read faster. Speed is optional. The one-pager is not.

1. One book at a time

Parallel reading feels like productivity. It is usually avoidance. Finish, or formally abandon. Both are honest. A half-read stack is not a mind.

2. Read with a question

Before page one, write the question the book is supposed to answer. “How do I avoid mistaking luck for skill?” is a question. “I should read Taleb because smart people mention him” is not.

3. Mark arguments, not vibes

Underline the claim, the mechanism, and the evidence. If you cannot find a mechanism, you found rhetoric. Rhetoric is allowed. Just label it.

4. Write the one-pager

After the last page: thesis in two sentences, three load-bearing ideas, one place the author is probably wrong, one action you will take in the next thirty days. If you skip this, the book evaporates in a week.

5. Cross the tracks

Judgment without capital is clever poverty. Capital without the body is a rich corpse. Power without character is a villain origin story. The point of five tracks is interference, not majors.

6. Distrust summaries — including these

The pages here are orientation. They exist so you do not start the wrong book at the wrong time. They are not the education. The education is the primary text plus the work you do to it.
