About

College is a sorting hat. It is not a monopoly on knowing things.

A note on what this is and what it is not.

A degree can be useful. It signals conscientiousness, buys you a network, and sometimes teaches a craft. What it does not do is guarantee that you can think, or that you have read the books that would have taught you to think.

This site exists because the other options are worse than they look. Universities sell a four-year social experience wrapped around a thin reading list. The internet sells summaries, threads, and people who have never finished the book they are explaining. Neither is a curriculum.

A curriculum is a sequence. First principles before jargon. Primary texts before commentary. Difficulty that compounds. The finance version of this idea already exists; this is the same shape pointed at a wider life — how to judge, how capital works, how a body fails, how power actually behaves, and how a person is supposed to live while all of that is happening.

What you will find here

  • Tracks, not majors. You pick a problem, not a department.
  • Guides, not substitutes. Each page is a map of a book: why it matters, what to watch for, what to do with it. The book is still the point.
  • Levels. Start at 1. Do not pretend you started at 4.
  • No email wall, no course upsell, no certificate.

What you will not find

  • The full text of copyrighted books. Buy them, borrow them, or use a library.
  • A claim that reading makes you an expert. Reading makes you less easily fooled. Expertise is reps.
  • Political catechism. If a book is here, it is here because it pays rent in clarity, not because it flatters a side.
This began as a private reading year — a push toward a hundred books across investing, medicine, history, philosophy, and the mental models that stitch them together. The public version is the same list, ordered so a stranger could walk it without already knowing the author.

How to use it

Pick one track. Read the Level 1 guide, then the book, then write one page in your own words: what would change if the author were right. Then go to the next book. If you skip the writing, you did not read it. You visited it.
